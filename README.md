# Making the new library of Serine Protease

Serine Proteases constitute a family of very important
enzymes with an important role in many physiological and
pathological pathways.

Their main function is to catalyze the **proteolysis of
peptidic bonds**. However, they are endowed with a
particular functional feature, that of **specificity to a P1
site**. Not only they are able to recognize a pattern and to
cleave after it (i.e. TEV proteases), but they are specific
pretty much just to the amino acid adjacent to the bond to
be cleaved.

There are very few examples of documented cleavage events.
Our goal is to characterize more serine proteases. A first
approach at testing specificities is to test natural
sequences. However, they come with the drawback of having
very different optimal conditions for expression and
activity.

## DMS

A **deep mutational scan** can be performed. This consists
in making all the one point mutants. The expression and
activity conditions will be more homogeneous. However, we
would like to go further from the wildtype.

## Sectors

We can use a prior knowledge about serine proteases in order
to try to create more variety in the variants we assay. It
is documented that serine proteases have sets of residues
that coevolve nearly independently. One of these sets,
called the **red sector**, is believed to play an important
role in determining specificity.
